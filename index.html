Natural Order

A Nature Guide to Mathematics

Mathematics inspired by the patterns, growth, and forms of the living world

The goal of this book let interesting patterns and forms in nature be an inspiration to learn about different branches of mathematics that connect to those phenomena. The book is for the mathematically curious and assumes nothing more than a background in high school algebra. Section one of each chapter introduce a series of natural phenomena relating to the chapter theme. Section two explore the mathematics need to understand the patterns that are observed. Section three offers a serious of vignettes of deeper mathematical topics that dig deeper into the mathematical concepts established in section three. Section four provides a series of explorations that give readers a chance to dig into the topics themselves.

Part I

Patterns and the Dynamics of Life

Chapter 1Number Patterns in Nature

Sequences in petals, pine cones, and leaves — recursive rules, formulas for the nth term, sums, and limits.

Chapter 2Natural Dynamics

Patterns as the record of processes played out over time — linear and exponential change, fixed points, cobweb diagrams, and the logistic map.

Chapter 3 Cycles and Periodicity

In development Nature's clocks — period, frequency, and phase; sine and cosine as models; phase-plane orbits and predator–prey cycles.

Chapter 4 Chance and Change

In development Randomness in living systems — probability, random walks and diffusion, Markov chains, and noisy dynamics.

Part II

Growth and Form

Chapter 5 Size and Shape

Why size matters — the square–cube law, power laws on log–log plots, dimensional analysis, and how proportions change across species.

Chapter 6 Gnomons

Shapes that grow into themselves — similar figures, the golden ratio, and the spirals traced by repeated growth.

Chapter 7 Optimal Form

In development Nature's shapes as solutions to optimization problems — extremes without calculus, Murray's law, and efficient packing.

Chapter 8 Symmetry

In development Transformations and invariance — the symmetry of organisms, strip and wallpaper patterns, and symmetry breaking.

Part III

Geometry of Life

Chapter 9 Spirals

Archimedean, logarithmic, and Fermat spirals in polar coordinates; spirals that emerge from density waves and from dynamical systems.

Chapter 10 Phyllotaxis

In development The golden angle, Fibonacci spirals, and simple models of how plants arrange their leaves and seeds.

Chapter 11 Branching

In development Trees, lungs, rivers, and blood vessels as branching structures — Murray's law, L-systems, and scaling with branching depth.

Part IV

Emergence

Chapter 12 Fractals

In development Coastlines without length, fractals built by iteration, and measuring fractal dimension in living structures.

Chapter 13 Filling Space — Tiling

In development Nature's mosaics — regular and semi-regular tilings, Voronoi diagrams, and aperiodic tilings.

Chapter 14 Cellular Automata

In development Local rules, global patterns — the Game of Life, elementary automata, and the pigment patterns of seashells.

Chapter 15 Self-Organization

In development Order without a blueprint — feedback, swarm behavior, and phase transitions in living systems.
